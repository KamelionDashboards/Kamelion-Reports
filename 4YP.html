Your Action Plan — June 2026
Priority
Activate your remaining 2 participants personally
At this scale, a direct personal invitation is the most effective activation method available. A text, a conversation in a session, or a peer-to-peer nudge from one of your existing active participants will almost certainly be enough. Waiting for them to activate independently is less likely to work — a personal touch makes the difference at small cohort sizes.
Before your next session, reach out individually to the 2 participants who haven't logged in. Frame it positively: "The others have already set their first goals — would you like to set yours this week?" Keep it low-pressure and personal.
Priority
Run a challenge session in June
Challenges haven't been introduced yet — and across every organisation using Kamelion, they consistently produce the highest engagement numbers when introduced through a guided session. For a youth programme, challenges can be framed as personal growth tasks that directly connect to your programme's goals. Even a short 10-minute introduction session could unlock significant activity next month.
Introduce challenges in your next group session. Walk participants through the challenge library, ask them to pick one that resonates, and set a date to check in on their progress. Make it feel like a programme activity, not a platform task.
Priority
Grow the goal-setting habit — build on the 100% record
3 goals, 3 completed — a perfect start. But 3 goals across a month is relatively low for 3 active participants. The opportunity is to make goal-setting a regular part of your programme rhythm: set a goal at the start of each session or fortnight, check in on progress, celebrate completions. The 100% record shows participants respond when given the structure to engage.
Build a brief goal-setting moment into every session — 5 minutes at the start. "What's one thing you want to achieve or work on before we meet next time?" Log it on Kamelion together. Review it at the next session before setting a new one.
Monitor
Encourage daily or every-other-day mood check-ins
5 moods from 3 participants over a month is a start — but the real value of mood tracking comes from consistency. Participants who check in regularly develop greater self-awareness and give the platform more data to work with. At this cohort size, a simple facilitator prompt at the start or end of each session can normalise the habit quickly.
At the end of each session, ask everyone to log a mood before they leave. Make it a 10-second ritual: "Before you go, just tap how you're feeling today." Over time this becomes automatic rather than something that needs to be prompted.
Monitor
Introduce a weekly journal prompt
3 journal entries is a positive start, but the habit needs reinforcing. For young people in a programme context, journaling works best when it's framed as part of the session experience rather than something to do independently. A single open-ended prompt — shared verbally or on a whiteboard — gives participants something to respond to without the blank-page barrier.
Each week, offer a simple reflection prompt: "Write 3 sentences about something you found difficult or interesting this week." It doesn't need to be long — the habit of writing matters more than the volume at this stage.
Keep Going
Use next month's report to show progress to stakeholders
By June, with more participants active, more goals set, and challenges introduced, the data picture will be significantly richer. That progression — from month one to month two — is a compelling story for funders, trustees, or programme partners who want evidence that participants are engaging with a structured wellbeing tool. Starting to document this journey now means you'll have a clear narrative to share.
Keep this report as your baseline. When June's report is available, place the two side by side to show growth. Even modest improvements — a few more goals, more mood check-ins, first challenges — tell a powerful story about programme impact.
