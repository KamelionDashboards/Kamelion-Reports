Your Action Plan — June 2026
Priority
Restart the goal-setting session in June — post-exams
The 41-goal, 100%-completion session from last month is the single most powerful piece of evidence you have that Alde Valley students respond to Kamelion when given structure. Letting that momentum fade without a follow-up is the most significant missed opportunity in the data. A post-exam restart — with a "what do you want to achieve before summer?" framing — could re-engage all 41 students immediately.
Speak to the teacher or pastoral lead who ran the last goal session. Schedule a follow-up session in the first two weeks of June. Frame it positively: "You all hit your goals last time — let's set new ones for the end of term."
Priority
Re-engage your 40 dormant activated students
40 students have accounts and previously engaged — they are significantly easier to re-activate than new students. They've already logged in, set goals, and completed challenges. A simple, direct prompt through form time, email, or a teacher mention could bring many of them back. The barrier isn't awareness — it's habit. Give them a reason to return in June.
Ask form tutors to mention Kamelion in morning registration next week — not as a push, just a reminder. "Log a mood this week and set one goal for the last half term." Keep it simple and specific.
Priority
Plan a September activation push now
509 students have unused licences. The best time to activate them is September — when routines reset, students are motivated, and new habits are easiest to form. Planning now means you can brief form tutors, schedule PSHE sessions, and communicate with students before the year begins. A structured September launch could transform Alde Valley's numbers overnight.
Before the end of term, agree a September activation plan with your Kamelion lead. Set a target — even 50 new students (taking you from 41 to 91 activated) would more than double your monitoring reach and engagement data.

Introduce journaling in September as a structured habit
All-time journal usage is 6 entries from 41 students — almost zero. But journaling is one of the highest-impact wellbeing tools available, particularly for students navigating transition, stress, or emotional uncertainty. Introducing it as a structured habit from the start of Year 7 — or as part of a PSHE programme — has the potential to become one of Alde Valley's strongest long-term outcomes.
Include a weekly journal prompt in your September Kamelion rollout plan. Something as simple as "3 minutes, one question, once a week" in form time can establish the habit quickly.
