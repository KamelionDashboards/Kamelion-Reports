Follow up on the 3 students who raised SOS topics
Three students flagged concerns in the very first month — sleep, friendship difficulties, and something unspecified. These students trusted the platform enough to ask for help almost immediately. Act Now
Run a goal-setting session in June — it's the missing piece
Every feature except Goals has been used in month one. Goals are consistently the highest-impact feature across all schools — with completion rates between 79% and 100% wherever they've been introduced through a structured session. Given the challenge session on 13 May worked so well, the same format applied to goal-setting is very likely to produce strong results immediately.
Speak to whoever ran the 13 May challenge session. Adapt the same format for goal-setting: a brief guided session where each student sets one goal for the half term. Frame it: "What's one thing you want to achieve or improve before the summer?"
Monitor
Find out who your early adopters are — and why
Two students have done 47 challenges each and written 19.5 journals each in month one. They are exceptionally engaged. Understanding what drew them in — whether it's a teacher prompt, personal motivation, or something about how the platform was introduced — is the most valuable information you can gather right now. That answer is your activation strategy for every other student.
Talk to your most active students informally. Ask what they've been using on Kamelion, how they found it, and what keeps them coming back. Their answer will tell you more about how to grow engagement than any data report.
Monitor
Replicate the 13 May session — make it a regular event
55 challenges in one day is the product of a structured session, not organic individual use. This is the most effective activation event in the data — and it happened in the very first month. Making it a regular event (once per half term, for example) would sustain challenge engagement at the levels you've already demonstrated are possible.
Schedule a challenge session for June — ideally with a wider group of students. Use the same format that worked on 13 May. Consider whether this could become a regular form time or PSHE activity each half term.
Build On
Protect and grow the journaling habit
19.5 journal entries per student in month one is extraordinary. This habit — if sustained — is one of the strongest predictors of long-term wellbeing improvement available to young people. The students who've built this practice deserve recognition, and the platform is providing them with a private, safe space to reflect. The task is ensuring this doesn't fade as the novelty of a new platform settles.
Consider introducing a weekly journal prompt through form time or PSHE — something open-ended that gives all students a reason to write. The goal isn't to mandate journaling, but to keep the invitation open and visible for those who benefit from it.
