What the Data Is Telling You
The 9 May group session changed everything
30 challenges in one day is almost certainly a structured classroom or form time session — not organic individual use. This is one of the most effective activation patterns in the data. A single guided group session produced more challenge activity than the preceding months combined. This is the model to repeat.
30 challenges · 1 day
NHS High School's community is a standout
253 community interactions from 11 students in a single month. That's an average of 23 interactions per student — far beyond what any other cohort is achieving. These students are using the platform to talk to each other, not just track themselves. A peer-to-peer culture is forming, and it's rare at this scale.
253 interactions · 11 students

Act Now
Check in on your 3 Amber-status students
Kamelion has flagged 3 students with Amber status — meaning multiple behavioural signals have triggered a concern. This isn't necessarily a crisis, but it's the platform telling you these students are showing patterns that warrant a conversation. At Amber, a pastoral check-in is appropriate before they move towards Red.
Ask your Kamelion lead to identify the 3 Amber students. Brief your pastoral team and make time for each to speak with someone they trust this week. Document the conversation.
Act Now
Replicate the 9 May group session — soon
Whatever happened on 9 May produced 30 challenge starts in a single day. That's almost certainly a structured group session — form time, PSHE, or a class activity. Replicating this once per half term could sustain the challenge momentum that the all-time data shows these students are capable of. The model is proven; it just needs repeating.
Speak to whichever member of staff led the 9 May session. Ask what they did and whether they'd be willing to run it again next half term — ideally with a different year group to broaden activation.
